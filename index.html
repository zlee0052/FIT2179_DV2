Malaysia’s Tourism Rebound Was Strong — But Uneven

In 2024, Malaysia’s tourism recovery was clear across arrivals, spending, hotel demand, and air connectivity. Yet the rebound was not evenly spread across source markets, spending categories, and states.

Part 1

The rebound was visible in arrivals and entry routes

Malaysia’s recovery first appears in the return of foreign visitors and the gateways they used to enter the country.

1. Tourism returned strongly in 2024

Foreign visitor arrivals were higher in every month of 2024 than in 2023. The recovery was especially visible toward the end of the year, with December recording the highest monthly arrivals.

2. Visitors mainly arrived by land and air

Land and air were the dominant entry modes for foreign visitors. Land travel reflected the importance of regional neighbours, while air arrivals highlighted the role of restored international flight connectivity.

Part 2

The recovery was shaped by different markets and spending patterns

Visitor recovery was not only about how many people arrived. Different source markets contributed different levels of growth, value, and spending impact.

3. Regional neighbours remained Malaysia’s core markets

ASEAN and nearby Asian markets formed the backbone of Malaysia’s visitor arrivals. Singapore remained the dominant source market, showing the importance of cross-border travel in the recovery.

4. Some markets recovered much faster than others

The recovery was not equal across all major visitor markets. China and India stood out as strong rebound markets, showing that growth came from more than just Malaysia’s largest neighbouring sources.

5. Visitor spending benefited specific sectors

Tourism recovery was not only about arrivals. Shopping, accommodation, and food and beverages captured the largest shares of spending, showing where visitor activity flowed into the economy.

6. Visitor volume and value told different stories

Some markets sent many visitors, while others generated higher receipts relative to their arrival numbers. Bubble size shows average spending per visitor, highlighting the difference between high-volume and high-value markets.

Part 3

The rebound was uneven across places and supported by infrastructure

The final section shifts from national totals to geography and capacity, showing how hotel demand and aviation links shaped the recovery across Malaysia.

7. Hotel demand varied across Malaysia

This choropleth map uses hotel occupancy rate, a percentage, rather than raw hotel room totals. Darker states had stronger hotel demand, showing that recovery intensity differed across Malaysia.

8. Hotel guests clustered in key states

A proportional symbol map is used because hotel guests are absolute counts, not normalised values. Larger circles indicate states with more hotel guests, while colour shows the share of foreign guests.

9. Domestic guests also shaped state tourism

The heatmap shows how each state’s hotel market was split between domestic and foreign guests. Darker cells indicate a larger share, revealing that many states relied heavily on domestic travellers.

10. Air connectivity supported the rebound

Weekly seat capacity shows how Malaysia reconnected with international regions. Upward slopes indicate stronger flight access in 2024, helping explain how visitor recovery was supported by transport capacity.

Conclusion

Malaysia’s tourism recovery was strong, but not uniform

Malaysia’s 2024 tourism rebound was clear across arrivals, spending, hotel demand, and air connectivity. However, the recovery was uneven: nearby markets delivered volume, selected Asian markets drove growth, domestic guests remained important across many states, and hotel demand varied geographically.
